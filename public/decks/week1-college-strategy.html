Reality check 💡

Where is a full ride easiest?

  • 1. CUNY / SUNY (in-state) — Excelsior + TAP + Pell = $0 for most NYC families.
  • 2. Wealthy private + Ivies — meet 100% of need; under ~$100K often free.
  • 3. Top liberal arts — no-loan aid + QuestBridge full rides.
  • 4. HBCUs — affordable but smaller endowments; expect to stack outside scholarships.

Lesson: apply to a mix — the cheapest path is often a name you didn't expect.

Choosing where to go 🧭

What to think about before you commit

Once the acceptances are in, don't just chase the ranking. Weigh best fit over prestige, have honest money conversations, and don't rush the decision. A helpful frame: the "Six F's" of college fit.

Focus 🎯

Academics & priorities — your major, class sizes, graduation rates, plus food, dorms, location.

Fellowship 🤝

Your people — community, diversity, affinity. Will you feel affirmed and challenged?

Financial 💰

Compare real net cost across 4 years. Prestige is relative; debt is not.

Future 🚀

Advising, alumni network, career outcomes, accreditation.

Family 🏠

Distance from home, family needs, travel logistics, traditions.

Feeling 💫

The gut check — do you feel excited, safe, and pulled toward this place?

College Strategy

What admissions actually weigh

  • GPA + rigor — grades in challenging classes you can handle.
  • Extracurriculars — depth and impact beat a long list.
  • Essays — your voice, your story, your "why."
  • Recommendations — teachers who know you well.
  • Fit — they want students who'll thrive there.
Build a balanced list

Reach · Target · Likely

Reach

Dream schools. Your stats are below or at the low end. Apply to a few.

Target

Your numbers fit the typical admit. The heart of your list.

Likely

Strong odds + you'd happily attend. Always include some.

Aim for ~8–12 schools spread across all three.

Paying for it

FAFSA + CSS Profile

FAFSA

  • Free federal aid form
  • Grants, loans, work-study
  • Required by every school

CSS Profile

  • Used by many private schools
  • Deeper financial picture
  • More institutional aid

Cost is a strategy question — never rule a school out before you see your aid.

Part 2 · Personal Narrative

The essay is not a résumé

Your grades and activities are already on the application. The essay's job is to make an admissions officer feel who you are. That comes from a story.

  • 650 words, one human, one voice.
  • They read thousands — yours stands out by being specific.
  • Show growth, not perfection.
Getting started · Harvard student tips

How to get unstuck

The scariest part is the blank page. You don't beat it by waiting for the perfect idea — you beat it by writing.

  • Make it uniquely you. If 1,000 essays got dropped on the floor, your family should be able to pick out yours by its voice.
  • Pick a topic that matters to you — not the most dramatic or "impressive" one. The McDonald's run with your friends can beat the trip to the moon.
  • Just start writing. The first draft can be stinky. Getting words down is how the essay finds its shape.
  • Write a lot, then trim. Pour it all out, then whittle down to 650 words.
  • Rewrite, then show it to people. Nobody nails it in one draft. Read it out loud; hand it to someone you trust.

Brainstorm · The 3-part story

Give your story a shape

A strong personal statement usually moves through three beats — split your ~650 words roughly evenly across them.

1 · Challenge + effect

What did you face, and how did it actually affect your life?

2 · What I did about it

The actions you took — big or small — to respond.

3 · What I learned

How you grew, what it means, and where it points you next.

💡 Warm-up (15 min): jot down the challenges you've faced, how each one made you feel, and what you needed to get through it. That list is the raw material for your essay.

From the video

The 5 keys to a great narrative

  • Pick a defining moment — small + everyday is fine.
  • Show growth — what changed in how you think.
  • Be specific — details, dialogue, senses. Show, don't tell.
  • Sound like you — not what you think they want.
  • Connect to the future — who you'll be on campus.
Remember

"The best topic isn't the most dramatic — it's the most you." Show a moment. Reflect. Reveal who you are.
