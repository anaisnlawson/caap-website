✈️ Part 1

Fly-In Programs

Some colleges will pay to bring you to campus — because they want students like you there.

The basics

What is a fly-in program?

A college covers the cost for prospective students to visit campus in person — so you can experience college life before deciding where to attend.

🚌 Travel

Flights, trains, or buses to get you there — that's where "fly-in" comes from.

🍽️ Meals

Food is covered while you're on campus for the visit.

🛏️ Lodging

A place to stay, usually in the dorms with current students.

On the visit

What actually happens there

These are usually 1–2 day (and night) visits, packed with a real taste of campus life.

  • Sit in on real classes and see how professors teach.
  • Get a campus tour and stay overnight in the dorms.
  • Meet current students — sometimes your future best friend. 👋
  • Attend financial aid + application info sessions.
  • Go to social events and feel the campus culture firsthand.
Why it's worth the effort

It's a win-win

🎓 What the school gets

  • A shot at the right-fit applicants
  • A more diverse, engaged community
  • Students who've seen it and love it

🙋 What you get

  • A free look before a huge decision
  • Proof it's the right place — or not
  • Demonstrated interest on your app

Finding out a school isn't right up close is just as valuable as confirming that it is.

Who these are for

Should you apply? Probably yes.

Fly-ins exist to give campus access to students who otherwise might not get it. You're a strong fit if you're…

🌱 First-gen

No family members who've done the college process before you.

🏫 Under-resourced

Your high school can't offer a lot of college-app support.

💵 Limited funds

A trip to visit campus isn't realistic for your family right now.

Each school sets its own eligibility — read the criteria carefully before you apply.

How to apply

What most fly-in applications ask for

  • A completed fly-in application (separate from your college app).
  • Financial information for you and your family.
  • An essay on why you're interested + how you'd benefit — basically a "Why Us."
  • Letters of recommendation from a teacher or counselor.
  • Your high school transcript.

These are competitive — getting one is a big deal. Don't let the work scare you off. 💪

The fly-in essay

Write it like a "Why Us"

The #1 mistake is an essay you could paste to any school. Be specific — prove you did your homework.

❌ Too generic

  • "I want to experience campus life"
  • "It's a great, highly-ranked school"
  • Facts copied off the homepage

✓ Specific & personal

  • A named program, class, or professor
  • A club or tradition you'd join
  • Why the visit matters for your decision

Colgate in Focus · the writing

The actual essay prompts ✍️

Diversity · ≤250 words

Colgate students engage with people from many backgrounds, races, religions, and perspectives. Share the benefits you see in engaging with a diverse community of students, faculty, and staff at Colgate.

What inspires you · ≤250 words

Colgate students immerse themselves in pursuits that inspire them. Tell us what inspires you and why you want to pursue that at Colgate.

Fun facts · ≤13 words each

Quick finish-the-sentence lines, like: "I am fascinated by…", "In the future, I hope to…", and "I am drawn to Colgate because…"

Notice: the two long prompts are basically a "Why Us" + a "you" essay — exactly the writing we've practiced. Prompts change year to year, so always confirm the current ones on Colgate's site.

💰 Part 2

Scholarships

Free money for college that you never have to pay back. Let's go get it.

Know the difference

Scholarships vs. everything else

🎁 Scholarships

Free money. Never repaid. Based on merit, need, or a quality you have.

💵 Grants

Also free, usually based on financial need — often from the college or government.

🧑‍💻 Work-study

Money you earn from a job while you're enrolled in college.

⚠️ Loans

Borrowed money that must be repaid, usually with interest. Not free.

Chase the free money first. Every scholarship dollar is a dollar you don't borrow.

Where scholarships come from

Three main sources

🏛️ Institutional

From the colleges themselves — the biggest source. Often automatic with your application.

🌎 Private / national

Companies, foundations, and nonprofits. Bigger awards, more applicants.

🏘️ Local

Businesses, clubs, and community groups near you. Smaller — but way less competition.

Hot tip: read the financial aid + scholarship page of every college you apply to. Don't leave money on the table.

What readers reward

Merit, need, or something that's just… you

Anyone can create a scholarship for anything. They're commonly based on:

  • Academic merit — top of the pool in academics, athletics, or the arts.
  • Merit + a quality — great grades and a specific talent or trait.
  • A quality, talent, or characteristic — sometimes on its own.
  • Financial need — helping students who need it most.

Think broadly about all your qualities — background, hobbies, heritage, where a parent works. There's a scholarship for almost everything.

How to find them · part 1

Build your list

1
Start early
Some open before grade 12. Grade 12? Tons have spring deadlines — keep going.
2
Think broadly
List every positive attribute you have, then search databases with those keywords.
3
Do research
Think local AND national. Use trusted sources. Never pay for a scholarship search.
4
Fit over quantity
Prioritize ones that feel tailor-made for you before the giant general ones.

Read the details
Meet every requirement exactly. Hyper-specific scholarships often go unclaimed.
—

Easy to apply = lots of competition. Specific + effort = better odds for you.

Protect yourself

Spot the scams 🚩

❌ Red flags — walk away

  • Asks you to pay a fee to apply or search
  • "Guaranteed" to win — no one can promise that
  • Wants your SSN or bank info up front
  • Pressures you to "act now"

✓ Legit scholarships

  • Always free to apply
  • Clear criteria and a real organization
  • Found via trusted databases or your school
  • Never ask for sensitive financial logins
Good to know

Outside scholarships + your aid

You must report all outside scholarships to your school. Here's the key formula:

Cost of Attendance − Expected Family Contribution = Calculated Need

  • If there's a gap in your aid, outside scholarships usually fill it first — great news.
  • If your need is already fully met, new scholarships may reduce loans first (also good!).
  • You generally can't be awarded more than your total cost of attendance.
Remember

Cost should never decide where you go. Fly-ins get you there. Scholarships help you stay. Start early, stay organized, and keep applying.
